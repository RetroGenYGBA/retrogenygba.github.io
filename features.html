Features and settings

The main features, and what the performance settings do.

Free and premium

Playing, in-game saves, save states, cheats, controllers, on-screen buttons and screen orientation are all free. These are premium features:

  • Fast forward: 1.5x, 2x and 4x (0.5x and 1x are free)
  • Rewind: hold the rewind button to step the game back
  • Turbo: hold a button to press it repeatedly, at a speed you choose
  • Filters: LCD, scanlines, color correction, xBRZ, HQ4x, ScaleFX, FSR and more
  • Memory search: find values yourself and make cheats

You can unlock them for free for a while right in the app, or buy the one-time unlock to keep them for good.

Display

  • Filters: switch them from the game menu, per game.
  • LCD ghosting: emulates the slow response of the original screen. Some games flicker sprites every other frame to fake transparency; this makes them look right.
  • Fast rendering: renders the screen one scanline at a time instead of pixel by pixel. Much faster, identical picture. On by default.
  • Multithreaded rendering: runs emulation and rendering in parallel on two CPU cores. Requires Fast rendering.

The game menu: speed, renderer and the performance switches can be changed without leaving the game.

Performance and battery

  • Continuous CPU execution: skips checks between instructions when nothing is happening. Faster and lighter on the battery, with exactly the same results. Off automatically while linked.
  • Power saving: avoids redrawing identical frames and similar savings, so the phone runs cooler. Can be set per game.

You can switch these from the game menu's display section without leaving the game. If anything looks or runs oddly, turn these switches off first.

Settings › Performance.

Sound

  • High-quality audio: finer resampling, cleaner in headphones.
  • Handheld speaker sound: mono, with the lows and highs trimmed like the built-in speaker, for that original sound.

Controllers and cartridge extras

  • Gamepad & keyboard buttons: rebind anything; one GBA button can have several keys.
  • Cartridge rumble: games with a rumble cartridge (like Pokémon Pinball: Ruby & Sapphire) vibrate the phone or controller.
  • Sensor cartridges: games with a tilt, gyro or solar sensor use your phone's own sensors. See the next section.

Sensor cartridges

Some cartridges had a sensor built right into them, so you had to step into the sun, tilt or twist the handheld to play. RetroGenY recognizes these games and swaps in your phone's own sensors automatically, with nothing to set up. When the game starts, it tells you which sensor is in use, or lets you know if your phone doesn't have the one the game needs.

  • Sunlight (Boktai 1, 2 and 3): your phone's light sensor stands in for the cartridge's solar sensor. The brighter the light, the more sunshine in the game: head outside or shine a lamp at the top of the screen and watch the sun gauge fill up. The light sensor usually sits next to the front camera, so keep your fingers off it. Phones without a light sensor get a medium amount of sun.
  • Tilt (Yoshi Topsy-Turvy, Koro Koro Puzzle): uses your phone's accelerometer, so you tilt the phone just like the original to spin the level or roll your character. However you're holding the phone when the game starts counts as level; to recalibrate, leave the game and start it again. Portrait and landscape both work, since the direction follows the screen.
  • Gyro (WarioWare: Twisted!): uses your phone's gyroscope. Turn the phone in the plane of the screen, like a steering wheel.
  • GBC tilt cartridges (Kirby Tilt 'n' Tumble and others): tilt controls through the accelerometer too, and the cartridge's save data is kept as normal.

With a controller connected, the sensors still read the phone itself, so tilt games are best played holding the phone. Sensors pause when the app goes to the background and pick up again when you return, so they don't drain your battery.

RetroAchievements

Turn it on in Settings › RetroAchievements and log in with your retroachievements.org account (free to sign up). When you start a game, the app recognizes it and tells you how many achievements it has. Unlocks pop up as you play, and "Achievements" in the game menu shows the full list and your progress.

  • RetroAchievements only recognizes the game versions it lists (it checks a checksum of the game file), so translations and ROM hacks usually have no achievements.
  • While it's on, cheats and memory editing pause so achievements stay fair. Fast forward, rewind and save states still work.
  • Unlocks earned offline are saved and sent once you're back online.
  • RetroAchievements is free.

Save states and rewind

Every save state slot has a thumbnail, and you can undo a load. Rewind needs some memory; if there isn't enough, the app tells you it couldn't turn on.

The save state menu: a new slot, the auto-save and your earlier slots.
